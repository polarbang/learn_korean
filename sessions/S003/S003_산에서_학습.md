### 오다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **오**다 | o-da |
| Present | **와**요 | wa-yo |
| Past | **왔**어요 | wa-sseo-yo |
| Future | **올** 거예요 | ol kkeo-ye-yo |

Rain "comes" in Korean: 비가 와요 = it's raining.

**Examples**
1. 날씨가 좋아요. 그래서 산에 **왔어요**.
   *nal-ssi-ga jo-a-yo. geu-rae-seo sa-ne wa-sseo-yo.*
   → The weather is nice. So I came to the mountain.
2. 친구가 우리 집에 **와요**.
   *chin-gu-ga u-ri ji-be wa-yo.*
   → My friend comes to my house.
3. 내일 비가 **올 거예요**.
   *nae-il bi-ga ol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → It's going to rain tomorrow.

### 불다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **불**다 | bul-da |
| Present | **불**어요 | bu-reo-yo |
| Past | **불**었어요 | bu-reo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **불** 거예요 | bul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** in the future form, 불 already ends in ㄹ, so it's just **불 거예요** (not 불을). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 산에 바람이 **불어요**.
   *sa-ne ba-ra-mi bu-reo-yo.*
   → The wind is blowing on the mountain.
2. 어제 바람이 많이 **불었어요**.
   *eo-je ba-ra-mi ma-ni bu-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → It was very windy yesterday.
3. 오늘 밤에 바람이 **불 거예요**.
   *o-neul ba-me ba-ra-mi bul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → It's going to be windy tonight.

### 가다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **가**다 | ga-da |
| Present | **가**요 | ga-yo |
| Past | **갔**어요 | ga-sseo-yo |
| Future | **갈** 거예요 | gal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 표지판 방향으로 **가요**.
   *pyo-ji-pan bang-hyang-eu-ro ga-yo.*
   → I go the way the sign points.
2. 어제 친구하고 카페에 **갔어요**.
   *eo-je chin-gu-ha-go ka-pe-e ga-sseo-yo.*
   → I went to a café with a friend yesterday.
3. 주말에 바다에 **갈 거예요**.
   *ju-ma-re ba-da-e gal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to go to the beach this weekend.

### 걷다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **걷**다 | geot-tta |
| Present | **걸**어요 | geo-reo-yo |
| Past | **걸**었어요 | geo-reo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **걸**을 거예요 | geo-reul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 걷 + 어요 becomes **걸어요** (ㄷ changes to ㄹ). It's still the same word.

In the video you hear **걸어와요** = walk toward me (걷다 + 오다).

**Examples**
1. 아저씨가 맨발로 **걸어요**.
   *a-jeo-ssi-ga maen-bal-lo geo-reo-yo.*
   → A man is walking barefoot.
2. 어제 공원에서 많이 **걸었어요**.
   *eo-je gong-wo-ne-seo ma-ni geo-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → I walked a lot in the park yesterday.
3. 내일은 학교까지 **걸을 거예요**.
   *nae-i-reun hak-kkyo-kka-ji geo-reul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → Tomorrow I'll walk to school.

### 올라가다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **올라가**다 | ol-la-ga-da |
| Present | **올라가**요 | ol-la-ga-yo |
| Past | **올라갔**어요 | ol-la-ga-sseo-yo |
| Future | **올라갈** 거예요 | ol-la-gal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 계단을 **올라가요**.
   *gye-da-neul ol-la-ga-yo.*
   → I go up the stairs.
2. 엘리베이터가 없어요. 그래서 3층까지 **올라갔어요**.
   *el-li-be-i-teo-ga eop-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo sam-cheung-kka-ji ol-la-ga-sseo-yo.*
   → There's no elevator. So I walked up to the 3rd floor.
3. 주말에 산에 **올라갈 거예요**.
   *ju-ma-re sa-ne ol-la-gal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to climb a mountain this weekend.

### 쌓다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **쌓**다 | ssa-ta |
| Present | **쌓**아요 | ssa-a-yo |
| Past | **쌓**았어요 | ssa-a-sseo-yo |
| Future | **쌓**을 거예요 | ssa-eul kkeo-ye-yo |

In the video you hear **돌이 쌓여 있어요** = stones are piled up.

**Examples**
1. 저도 돌을 하나 **쌓아요**.
   *jeo-do do-reul ha-na ssa-a-yo.*
   → I stack one stone, too.
2. 책을 책상 위에 **쌓았어요**.
   *chae-geul chaek-ssang wi-e ssa-a-sseo-yo.*
   → I piled the books on the desk.
3. 상자를 방 안에 **쌓을 거예요**.
   *sang-ja-reul bang a-ne ssa-eul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to stack the boxes in the room.

### 내려가다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **내려가**다 | nae-ryeo-ga-da |
| Present | **내려가**요 | nae-ryeo-ga-yo |
| Past | **내려갔**어요 | nae-ryeo-ga-sseo-yo |
| Future | **내려갈** 거예요 | nae-ryeo-gal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 이제 산을 **내려가요**.
   *i-je sa-neul nae-ryeo-ga-yo.*
   → Now I go down the mountain.
2. 계단으로 1층까지 **내려갔어요**.
   *gye-da-neu-ro il-cheung-kka-ji nae-ryeo-ga-sseo-yo.*
   → I took the stairs down to the 1st floor.
3. 다섯 시에 산을 **내려갈 거예요**.
   *da-seot ssi-e sa-neul nae-ryeo-gal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll go down the mountain at five.

### 피다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **피**다 | pi-da |
| Present | **피**어요 | pi-eo-yo |
| Past | **피**었어요 | pi-eo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **필** 거예요 | pil kkeo-ye-yo |

People often shorten these: 피어요 → **펴요**, 피었어요 → **폈어요** (that's what you hear in the video).

**Examples**
1. 산에 꽃이 **피었어요**.
   *sa-ne kko-chi pi-eo-sseo-yo.*
   → Flowers have bloomed on the mountain.
2. 봄에는 꽃이 많이 **피어요**.
   *bo-me-neun kko-chi ma-ni pi-eo-yo.*
   → Lots of flowers bloom in spring.
3. 다음 주에 벚꽃이 **필 거예요**.
   *da-eum ju-e beot-kko-chi pil kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → The cherry blossoms will bloom next week.

### 앉다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **앉**다 | an-tta |
| Present | **앉**아요 | an-ja-yo |
| Past | **앉**았어요 | an-ja-sseo-yo |
| Future | **앉**을 거예요 | an-jeul kkeo-ye-yo |

For birds and insects, 앉다 means *to land* (on something).

**Examples**
1. 벌이 꽃에 **앉았어요**.
   *beo-ri kko-che an-ja-sseo-yo.*
   → A bee landed on the flower.
2. 의자에 **앉아요**.
   *ui-ja-e an-ja-yo.*
   → I sit on the chair.
3. 버스에서 창문 옆에 **앉을 거예요**.
   *beo-seu-e-seo chang-mun yeo-pe an-jeul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll sit by the window on the bus.

### 시원하다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **시원하**다 | si-won-ha-da |
| Present | **시원해**요 | si-won-hae-yo |
| Past | **시원했**어요 | si-won-hae-sseo-yo |

시원하다 is the *nice* kind of cool — a breeze, a cold drink. For cold weather, Korean uses 춥다.

**Examples**
1. 산은 바람이 **시원해요**.
   *sa-neun ba-ra-mi si-won-hae-yo.*
   → The breeze on the mountain is cool.
2. 아이스커피가 정말 **시원해요**.
   *a-i-seu-keo-pi-ga jeong-mal si-won-hae-yo.*
   → The iced coffee is really refreshing.
3. 어제 날씨가 **시원했어요**.
   *eo-je nal-ssi-ga si-won-hae-sseo-yo.*
   → The weather was cool yesterday.

### 목마르다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **목마르**다 | mong-ma-reu-da |
| Present | **목말라**요 | mong-mal-la-yo |
| Past | **목말랐**어요 | mong-mal-la-sseo-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 목마르 + 아요 becomes **목말라요** (an extra ㄹ appears). It's still the same word.

In the video you hear **목이 말라요** — the same meaning: 목 (throat) + 마르다 (to be dry).

**Examples**
1. 계단을 올라갔어요. **목말라요**.
   *gye-da-neul ol-la-ga-sseo-yo. mong-mal-la-yo.*
   → I climbed the stairs. I'm thirsty.
2. 운동했어요. 그래서 **목말라요**.
   *un-dong-hae-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo mong-mal-la-yo.*
   → I worked out. So I'm thirsty.
3. 어제 너무 **목말랐어요**.
   *eo-je neo-mu mong-mal-la-sseo-yo.*
   → I was so thirsty yesterday.

### 힘들다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **힘들**다 | him-deul-da |
| Present | **힘들**어요 | him-deu-reo-yo |
| Past | **힘들**었어요 | him-deu-reo-sseo-yo |

**Examples**
1. 경사가 가팔라요. 조금 **힘들어요**.
   *gyeong-sa-ga ga-pal-la-yo. jo-geum him-deu-reo-yo.*
   → The slope is steep. It's a little tiring.
2. 한국어 공부는 조금 **힘들어요**.
   *han-gu-geo gong-bu-neun jo-geum him-deu-reo-yo.*
   → Studying Korean is a little hard.
3. 어제 일이 많았어요. 정말 **힘들었어요**.
   *eo-je i-ri ma-na-sseo-yo. jeong-mal him-deu-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → I had a lot of work yesterday. It was really tough.

### 졸리다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **졸리**다 | jol-li-da |
| Present | **졸려**요 | jol-lyeo-yo |
| Past | **졸렸**어요 | jol-lyeo-sseo-yo |

In the video you hear **졸린가 봐요** = it looks sleepy (when you guess about someone else).

**Examples**
1. 고양이가 눈을 감아요. **졸려요**?
   *go-yang-i-ga nu-neul ga-ma-yo. jol-lyeo-yo?*
   → The cat closes its eyes. Is it sleepy?
2. 어제 늦게 잤어요. 그래서 **졸려요**.
   *eo-je neut-kke ja-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo jol-lyeo-yo.*
   → I went to bed late last night. So I'm sleepy.
3. 수업 시간에 너무 **졸렸어요**.
   *su-eop si-ga-ne neo-mu jol-lyeo-sseo-yo.*
   → I was so sleepy in class.

### 귀엽다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **귀엽**다 | gwi-yeop-tta |
| Present | **귀여워**요 | gwi-yeo-wo-yo |
| Past | **귀여웠**어요 | gwi-yeo-wo-sseo-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 귀엽 + 어요 becomes **귀여워요** (ㅂ changes to 우). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 고양이가 엄청 **귀여워요**.

3. 아기가 웃었어요. 정말 **귀여웠어요**.
   *a-gi-ga u-seo-sseo-yo. jeong-mal gwi-yeo-wo-sseo-yo.*
   → The baby smiled. It was so cute.

## Adverbs

| Korean | Meaning | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 계속 | continuously, keep (doing) | gye-sok |
| 못 | can't | mot |
| 지금 | now | ji-geum |
| 이제 | now (from this point on) | i-je |

### 계속

**Examples**
1. **계속** 오르막길이에요.
   *gye-sok o-reu-mak-kki-ri-e-yo.*
   → It keeps going uphill.
2. 아침부터 비가 **계속** 와요.
   *a-chim-bu-teo bi-ga gye-sok wa-yo.*
   → It's been raining since the morning.
3. 내년에도 한국어를 **계속** 공부할 거예요.
   *nae-nyeo-ne-do han-gu-geo-reul gye-sok gong-bu-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll keep studying Korean next year, too.

### 못

Put 못 right before the verb.

**Examples**
1. 저쪽으로는 **못** 가요.
   *jeo-jjo-geu-ro-neun mot kka-yo.*
   → You can't go that way.
2. 저는 수영을 **못** 해요.
   *jeo-neun su-yeong-eul mo tae-yo.*
   → I can't swim.
3. 어제 너무 바빴어요. 그래서 밥을 **못** 먹었어요.
   *eo-je neo-mu ba-ppa-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo ba-beul mon meo-geo-sseo-yo.*
   → I was so busy yesterday. So I couldn't eat.

### 지금

지금 = right now, at this moment.

**Examples**
1. **지금** 제 위치를 확인해요.
   *ji-geum je wi-chi-reul hwa-gin-hae-yo.*
   → I check where I am now.
2. **지금** 몇 시예요?
   *ji-geum myeot ssi-ye-yo?*
   → What time is it now?
3. 저는 **지금** 집에 있어요.
   *jeo-neun ji-geum ji-be i-sseo-yo.*
   → I'm at home now.

### 이제

이제 = now, when something changes from here ("OK, now let's…").

**Examples**
1. **이제** 산을 내려가요.
   *i-je sa-neul nae-ryeo-ga-yo.*
   → Now I head down the mountain.
2. 숙제를 다 했어요. **이제** 놀 거예요.
   *suk-jje-reul da hae-sseo-yo. i-je nol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I finished my homework. Now I'm going to play.

### 수다를 떨다

수다 means *chatter*. In the video you hear **수다를 떨고 있어요** = they are chatting (right now).

**Examples**
1. 아주머니 세 명이 **수다를 떨어요**.
   *a-ju-meo-ni se myeong-i su-da-reul tteo-reo-yo.*
   → Three women are chatting.
2. 어제 카페에서 친구하고 **수다를 떨었어요**.
   *eo-je ka-pe-e-seo chin-gu-ha-go su-da-reul tteo-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → I chatted with a friend at a café yesterday.
3. 주말에 친구들하고 **수다를 떨 거예요**.
   *ju-ma-re chin-gu-deul-ha-go su-da-reul tteol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to chat with my friends this weekend.

## Grammar Point: 이/가 있어요 (there is)

Use **있어요** to say something *is there* — perfect for a walk, when you point at what you see.
The thing you see takes **이** or **가**: after a final consonant (받침) use **이** (계단**이**), after a vowel use **가** (지도**가**).

> **[noun]이/가 있어요** = There is [noun].

To say *where*, put **[place]에** in front. For the past, use **있었어요** (there was).

| Next to the path | a railing | there is |
|---|---|---|
| 길 옆에 | 난간**이** | 있어요 |
| *gil yeo-pe* | *nan-ga-ni* | *i-sseo-yo* |

→ There's a railing next to the path.

**Examples**
1. 산에 계단**이** 많이 있어요.
   *sa-ne gye-da-ni ma-ni i-sseo-yo.*
   → There are lots of stairs on the mountain.
2. 가방 안에 텀블러**가** 있어요.
   *ga-bang a-ne teom-beul-leo-ga i-sseo-yo.*
   → There's a tumbler in my bag.
3. 집 앞에 고양이**가** 있었어요.
   *jip a-pe go-yang-i-ga i-sseo-sseo-yo.*
   → There was a cat in front of my house.
