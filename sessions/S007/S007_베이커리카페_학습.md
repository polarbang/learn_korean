# Ep. 7 · A Day at a Korean Bakery Café — Study Notes

Video: https://youtu.be/M7YwF3CB3Q4

## How to read this page

- **Sound** is an approximate guide to pronunciation, written in English letters. Listen to the video for the actual sound.
- **From the video** uses the narration with standard spelling. **Try it elsewhere** helps you use the same expression in a new situation.
- The verbs below use the dictionary form (ending in **-다**). In the video, you hear polite forms such as **찾아요** and **찾았어요**.

## The story in six sentences

1. 카페 근처 주차장에 주차를 해요. — I park in a parking lot near the café.
2. 먼저 자리를 맡으러 위층으로 올라가요. — First, I go upstairs to save a seat.
3. 종이를 쟁반에 깔고 빵을 골라요. — I put a sheet of paper on a tray and choose bread.
4. 소금빵을 하나 담고 커피를 시켜요. — I take one salt bread and order coffee.
5. 빵을 먹고 카페를 둘러봐요. — I eat the bread and look around the café.
6. 쓰레기는 버리고 쟁반은 반납대에 넣어요. — I throw away the trash and put the tray at the return station.

## 표현 (Useful expressions)

| Korean | Meaning | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 이미 다 팔렸어요. | It has already sold out. | i-mi da pal-lyeo-sseo-yo |
| 빵 다 먹었어요. | I finished the bread. | ppang da meo-geo-sseo-yo |
| 커피 다 마셨어요. | I finished the coffee. | keo-pi da ma-syeo-sseo-yo |
| 배가 불러요. | I'm full. | bae-ga bul-leo-yo |

Use **먹다** for food and **마시다** for drinks. **다** adds the idea of finishing it all. In **다 팔렸어요**, the sandwiches have all been sold.

## 문법 (Grammar)

### 1. N보다 + adjective — comparing things

Put **보다** after the thing you compare against. **더** (more) is optional when the comparison is already clear.

> **A는/은 B보다 길어요** = A is longer than B.

**From the video:** 벤치는 **의자보다 길어요**.  
*ben-chi-neun ui-ja-bo-da gi-reo-yo.*  
→ A bench is longer than a chair.

**Try it elsewhere:** 이 방은 저 방보다 **밝아요**.  
*i bang-eun jeo bang-bo-da bal-ga-yo.*  
→ This room is brighter than that room.

**Your turn:** ＿＿은/는 ＿＿보다 ＿＿어요/아요.

### 2. V-(으)면 — if; when

Add **-으면** after a verb stem ending in a consonant (except ㄹ), and **-면** after a vowel or ㄹ. Here it gives directions.

> **나가서 오른쪽으로 가면 돼요** = Go out, then turn right; that will do.

**From the video:** 나가서 **오른쪽으로 가면 돼요**.  
*na-ga-seo o-reun-jjo-geu-ro ga-myeon dwae-yo.*  
→ Go out and turn right.

**Try it elsewhere:** 계단을 **올라가면** 2층이 나와요.  
*gye-da-neul ol-la-ga-myeon i-cheung-i na-wa-yo.*  
→ If you go up the stairs, you'll reach the second floor.

**Your turn:** 카페에서 화장실은 어디예요? ＿＿면 돼요.

### 3. -아/어져 있어요 — something is in a resulting state

This pattern describes a state you can see now. In this video, the cream and salt have already been placed on the bread. Learn these as useful whole expressions.

| Expression | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 크림이 올려져 있어요 | Cream has been put on top. |
| 소금이 뿌려져 있어요 | Salt has been sprinkled on it. |
| 커튼이 쳐져 있어요 | The curtain is drawn. |

**From the video:** 빵 겉면에 **소금이 뿌려져 있어요**.  
*ppang geon-myeo-ne so-geu-mi ppu-ryeo-jyeo i-sseo-yo.*  
→ There is salt sprinkled on the outside of the bread.

**Try it elsewhere:** 케이크 위에 과일이 **올려져 있어요**.  
*ke-i-keu wi-e gwa-i-ri ol-lyeo-jyeo i-sseo-yo.*  
→ Fruit has been placed on top of the cake.

## Say it yourself

1. **Where would you sit?** 실내에 앉을까요, 야외에 앉을까요? → 저는 ＿＿에 앉을 거예요.
2. **What would you choose?** 어떤 빵을 담을까요? → 저는 ＿＿을/를 담을 거예요.
3. **What do you do at a café?** 카페에 가면 뭘 해요? → 저는 카페에 가면 ＿＿어요/아요.

**Example:** 저는 카페에 가면 커피를 마시면서 책을 읽어요.  
*jeo-neun ka-pe-e ga-myeon keo-pi-reul ma-si-myeon-seo chae-geul il-geo-yo.*  
→ When I go to a café, I read a book while drinking coffee.
