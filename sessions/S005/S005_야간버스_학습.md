# S005 · Taking a Night Bus in Korea — Study Notes

## How to read this page
- **Sound** = how the word sounds, written in English letters.
- Korean verbs and adjectives change their ending, but they are **still the same word**.
  The **bold part** stays the same — only the ending changes.

### 타다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **타**다 | ta-da |
| Present | **타**요 | ta-yo |
| Past | **탔**어요 | ta-sseo-yo |
| Future | **탈** 거예요 | tal kkeo-ye-yo |

Korean says 버스**를** 타다 — the bus, subway, taxi or escalator takes 을/를.

**Examples**
1. 지하철을 **타요**.
   *ji-ha-cheo-reul ta-yo.*
   → I take the subway.
2. 어제 택시를 **탔어요**.
   *eo-je taek-si-reul ta-sseo-yo.*
   → I took a taxi yesterday.
3. 내일 비행기를 **탈 거예요**.
   *nae-il bi-haeng-gi-reul tal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to take a plane tomorrow.

### 도착하다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **도착하**다 | do-cha-ka-da |
| Present | **도착해**요 | do-cha-kae-yo |
| Past | **도착했**어요 | do-cha-kae-sseo-yo |
| Future | **도착할** 거예요 | do-cha-kal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 버스 터미널에 **도착했어요**.
   *beo-seu teo-mi-neo-re do-cha-kae-sseo-yo.*
   → I arrived at the bus terminal.
2. 친구가 지금 공항에 **도착해요**.
   *chin-gu-ga ji-geum gong-hang-e do-cha-kae-yo.*
   → My friend is arriving at the airport now.
3. 세 시에 집에 **도착할 거예요**.
   *se si-e ji-be do-cha-kal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll get home at three.

### 나가다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **나가**다 | na-ga-da |
| Present | **나가**요 | na-ga-yo |
| Past | **나갔**어요 | na-ga-sseo-yo |
| Future | **나갈** 거예요 | na-gal kkeo-ye-yo |

In the video you see the sign **나가는 곳** = Exit (literally "the place where you go out").

**Examples**
1. 승강장으로 **나가요**.
   *seung-gang-jang-eu-ro na-ga-yo.*
   → I head out to the platform.
2. 아침에 일찍 **나갔어요**.
   *a-chi-me il-jjik na-ga-sseo-yo.*
   → I went out early in the morning.
3. 저녁에 친구하고 **나갈 거예요**.
   *jeo-nyeo-ge chin-gu-ha-go na-gal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going out with a friend this evening.

### 찍다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **찍**다 | jjik-tta |
| Present | **찍**어요 | jji-geo-yo |
| Past | **찍**었어요 | jji-geo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **찍**을 거예요 | jji-geul kkeo-ye-yo |

찍다 is what you do with a card, a ticket or a QR code: tap it or scan it. With a camera, it means to take a photo: 사진을 찍다.

**Examples**
1. 여기에 카드를 **찍어요**.
   *yeo-gi-e ka-deu-reul jji-geo-yo.*
   → I tap my card here.
2. 버스에서 티켓을 **찍었어요**.
   *beo-seu-e-seo ti-ke-seul jji-geo-sseo-yo.*
   → I scanned my ticket on the bus.
3. 산에서 사진을 많이 **찍을 거예요**.
   *sa-ne-seo sa-ji-neul ma-ni jji-geul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to take lots of photos on the mountain.

### 예매하다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **예매하**다 | ye-mae-ha-da |
| Present | **예매해**요 | ye-mae-hae-yo |
| Past | **예매했**어요 | ye-mae-hae-sseo-yo |
| Future | **예매할** 거예요 | ye-mae-hal kkeo-ye-yo |

예매하다 is for tickets: buses, trains, movies, concerts.

**Examples**
1. 앱으로 버스표를 **예매했어요**.
   *a-beu-ro beo-seu-pyo-reul ye-mae-hae-sseo-yo.*
   → I booked a bus ticket with an app.
2. 저는 영화표를 항상 미리 **예매해요**.
   *jeo-neun yeong-hwa-pyo-reul hang-sang mi-ri ye-mae-hae-yo.*
   → I always book movie tickets in advance.
3. 오늘 기차표를 **예매할 거예요**.
   *o-neul gi-cha-pyo-reul ye-mae-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to book a train ticket today.

### 출발하다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **출발하**다 | chul-bal-ha-da |
| Present | **출발해**요 | chul-bal-hae-yo |
| Past | **출발했**어요 | chul-bal-hae-sseo-yo |
| Future | **출발할** 거예요 | chul-bal-hal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 8시 20분이에요. 이제 버스가 **출발해요**.
   *yeo-deol si i-sip-bu-ni-e-yo. i-je beo-seu-ga chul-bal-hae-yo.*
   → It's 8:20. Now the bus is leaving.
2. 기차가 이미 **출발했어요**.
   *gi-cha-ga i-mi chul-bal-hae-sseo-yo.*
   → The train has already left.
3. 내일 아침 일곱 시에 **출발할 거예요**.
   *nae-il a-chim il-gop si-e chul-bal-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll set off at seven tomorrow morning.

### 걸리다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **걸리**다 | geol-li-da |
| Present | **걸려**요 | geol-lyeo-yo |
| Past | **걸렸**어요 | geol-lyeo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **걸릴** 거예요 | geol-lil kkeo-ye-yo |

For time: **[time]이/가 걸려요** = it takes [time].

**Examples**
1. 버스로 1시간 50분이 **걸려요**.
   *beo-seu-ro han si-gan o-sip-bu-ni geol-lyeo-yo.*
   → It takes 1 hour 50 minutes by bus.
2. 집에서 학교까지 10분 **걸려요**.
   *ji-be-seo hak-kkyo-kka-ji sip-bun geol-lyeo-yo.*
   → It takes 10 minutes from home to school.
3. 어제 숙제가 오래 **걸렸어요**.
   *eo-je suk-jje-ga o-rae geol-lyeo-sseo-yo.*
   → My homework took a long time yesterday.

### 기다리다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **기다리**다 | gi-da-ri-da |
| Present | **기다려**요 | gi-da-ryeo-yo |
| Past | **기다렸**어요 | gi-da-ryeo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **기다릴** 거예요 | gi-da-ril kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 사람들이 버스를 **기다려요**.
   *sa-ram-deu-ri beo-seu-reul gi-da-ryeo-yo.*
   → People are waiting for the bus.
2. 카페에서 친구를 **기다렸어요**.
   *ka-pe-e-seo chin-gu-reul gi-da-ryeo-sseo-yo.*
   → I waited for my friend at a café.
3. 여기에서 **기다릴 거예요**.
   *yeo-gi-e-seo gi-da-ril kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll wait here.

### 끄다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **끄**다 | kkeu-da |
| Present | **꺼**요 | kkeo-yo |
| Past | **껐**어요 | kkeo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **끌** 거예요 | kkeul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 끄 + 어요 becomes **꺼요** (the ㅡ drops out). It's still the same word.

The opposite is 켜다 (to turn on) — you see both on the night bus.

**Examples**
1. 버스가 출발해요. 그리고 불을 **꺼요**.
   *beo-seu-ga chul-bal-hae-yo. geu-ri-go bu-reul kkeo-yo.*
   → The bus leaves. And they turn off the lights.
2. 영화관에서 핸드폰을 **껐어요**.
   *yeong-hwa-gwa-ne-seo haen-deu-po-neul kkeo-sseo-yo.*
   → I turned off my phone at the movie theater.
3. 이제 텔레비전을 **끌 거예요**.
   *i-je tel-le-bi-jeo-neul kkeul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → Now I'm going to turn off the TV.

### 내리다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **내리**다 | nae-ri-da |
| Present | **내려**요 | nae-ryeo-yo |
| Past | **내렸**어요 | nae-ryeo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **내릴** 거예요 | nae-ril kkeo-ye-yo |

내리다 is the opposite of 타다: you get off a bus or subway. It also means *to lower* — in the video, 좌석을 뒤로 내려요 = I lean the seat back.

**Examples**
1. 이제 버스에서 **내려요**.
   *i-je beo-seu-e-seo nae-ryeo-yo.*
   → Now I get off the bus.
2. 서울역에서 **내렸어요**.
   *seo-ul-lyeo-ge-seo nae-ryeo-sseo-yo.*
   → I got off at Seoul Station.
3. 다음 역에서 **내릴 거예요**.
   *da-eum yeo-ge-seo nae-ril kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll get off at the next station.

### 눕다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **눕**다 | nup-tta |
| Present | **누워**요 | nu-wo-yo |
| Past | **누웠**어요 | nu-wo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **누울** 거예요 | nu-ul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 눕 + 어요 becomes **누워요** (ㅂ changes to 우). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 의자를 뒤로 내렸어요. 그리고 **누웠어요**.
   *ui-ja-reul dwi-ro nae-ryeo-sseo-yo. geu-ri-go nu-wo-sseo-yo.*
   → I leaned the seat back. And I lay down.
2. 피곤해요. 침대에 **누워요**.
   *pi-gon-hae-yo. chim-dae-e nu-wo-yo.*
   → I'm tired. I lie down on the bed.
3. 이따가 소파에 **누울 거예요**.
   *i-tta-ga so-pa-e nu-ul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to lie down on the sofa later.

### 자다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **자**다 | ja-da |
| Present | **자**요 | ja-yo |
| Past | **잤**어요 | ja-sseo-yo |
| Future | **잘** 거예요 | jal kkeo-ye-yo |

In the video you hear **누워서 자요** = I lie down and sleep.

**Examples**
1. 저는 버스에서 항상 **자요**.
   *jeo-neun beo-seu-e-seo hang-sang ja-yo.*
   → I always sleep on the bus.
2. 어제 열 시에 **잤어요**.
   *eo-je yeol si-e ja-sseo-yo.*
   → I went to bed at ten last night.
3. 오늘은 일찍 **잘 거예요**.
   *o-neu-reun il-jjik jal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to sleep early tonight.

### 쉬다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **쉬**다 | swi-da |
| Present | **쉬**어요 | swi-eo-yo |
| Past | **쉬**었어요 | swi-eo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **쉴** 거예요 | swil kkeo-ye-yo |

In the video you hear **쉬어야겠어요** = I'd better get some rest.

**Examples**
1. 숙소에 가요. 그리고 **쉬어요**.
   *suk-so-e ga-yo. geu-ri-go swi-eo-yo.*
   → I go to where I'm staying. And I rest.
2. 주말에 집에서 **쉬었어요**.
   *ju-ma-re ji-be-seo swi-eo-sseo-yo.*
   → I rested at home over the weekend.
3. 피곤해요. 그래서 오늘은 **쉴 거예요**.
   *pi-gon-hae-yo. geu-rae-seo o-neu-reun swil kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm tired. So I'll rest today.

## 부사 (Adverbs)

| Korean | Meaning | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 항상 | always | hang-sang |
| 미리 | in advance, ahead of time | mi-ri |
| 아직 | still, (not) yet | a-jik |
| 빨리 | fast, early, quickly | ppal-li |

### 항상

**Examples**
1. 한국 사람들은 에스컬레이터에서 **항상** 오른쪽에 서요.
   *han-guk sa-ram-deu-reun e-seu-keol-le-i-teo-e-seo hang-sang o-reun-jjo-ge seo-yo.*
   → Koreans always stand on the right on escalators.
2. 저는 아침에 **항상** 커피를 마셔요.
   *jeo-neun a-chi-me hang-sang keo-pi-reul ma-syeo-yo.*
   → I always drink coffee in the morning.

### 미리

**Examples**
1. 온라인으로 티켓을 **미리** 샀어요.
   *ol-la-i-neu-ro ti-ke-seul mi-ri sa-sseo-yo.*
   → I bought the ticket online in advance.
2. 내일 시험이 있어요. 그래서 **미리** 공부할 거예요.
   *nae-il si-heo-mi i-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo mi-ri gong-bu-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I have a test tomorrow. So I'm going to study ahead of time.

### 아직

With 안, 아직 means *not yet*.

**Examples**
1. 버스가 **아직** 안 왔어요.
   *beo-seu-ga a-jik an wa-sseo-yo.*
   → The bus hasn't come yet.
2. **아직** 밥을 안 먹었어요.
   *a-jik ba-beul an meo-geo-sseo-yo.*
   → I haven't eaten yet.
3. 친구가 **아직** 자요.
   *chin-gu-ga a-jik ja-yo.*
   → My friend is still sleeping.

### 빨리

**Examples**
1. 버스가 2분 **빨리** 도착했어요.
   *beo-seu-ga i-bun ppal-li do-cha-kae-sseo-yo.*
   → The bus arrived 2 minutes early.
2. 늦었어요! **빨리** 가요!
   *neu-jeo-sseo-yo! ppal-li ga-yo!*
   → We're late! Let's go, quick!
3. 친구가 밥을 **빨리** 먹어요.
   *chin-gu-ga ba-beul ppal-li meo-geo-yo.*
   → My friend eats fast.

## 표현 (Expressions)

| Korean | Meaning | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 배가 고프다 | to be hungry | bae-ga go-peu-da |
| 커튼을 치다 | to draw the curtain | keo-teu-neul chi-da |

### 배가 고프다

배 means *stomach*. To say "I'm hungry," say **배가 고파요** (bae-ga go-pa-yo).

**Examples**
1. 버스를 기다려요. **배가 고파요**.
   *beo-seu-reul gi-da-ryeo-yo. bae-ga go-pa-yo.*
   → I'm waiting for the bus. I'm hungry.
2. 아침을 안 먹었어요. 그래서 **배가 고파요**.
   *a-chi-meul an meo-geo-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo bae-ga go-pa-yo.*
   → I didn't eat breakfast. So I'm hungry.
3. 어제 정말 **배가 고팠어요**.
   *eo-je jeong-mal bae-ga go-pa-sseo-yo.*
   → I was really hungry yesterday.

### 커튼을 치다

치다 usually means *to hit*, but with a curtain it means to pull it closed.

**Examples**
1. 버스에서 **커튼을 쳐요**.
   *beo-seu-e-seo keo-teu-neul cheo-yo.*
   → I draw the curtain on the bus.
2. 방이 너무 밝아요. 그래서 **커튼을 쳤어요**.
   *bang-i neo-mu bal-ga-yo. geu-rae-seo keo-teu-neul cheo-sseo-yo.*
   → The room is too bright. So I drew the curtains.

## 문법 (Grammar): -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 (can)

Put **-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요** on a verb to say you *can* do something — you hear it again and again at the bus terminal.
After a final consonant (받침) use **-을 수 있어요** (읽**을 수 있어요**), after a vowel use **-ㄹ 수 있어요** (사다 → **살 수 있어요**).

> **[verb]-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요** = I / You can [verb].

For *can't*, use **-(으)ㄹ 수 없어요**, or put 못 before the verb.

| At the ticket office | tickets | can buy |
|---|---|---|
| 매표소에서 | 티켓을 | **살 수 있어요** |
| *mae-pyo-so-e-seo* | *ti-ke-seul* | *sal ssu i-sseo-yo* |

→ You can buy tickets at the ticket office.

**Examples**
1. 의자를 뒤로 **내릴 수 있어요**.
   *ui-ja-reul dwi-ro nae-ril ssu i-sseo-yo.*
   → You can lean the seat back.
2. 저는 한국어를 조금 **읽을 수 있어요**.
   *jeo-neun han-gu-geo-reul jo-geum il-geul ssu i-sseo-yo.*
   → I can read a little Korean.
3. 지하철로 터미널에 **갈 수 있어요**.
   *ji-ha-cheol-lo teo-mi-neo-re gal ssu i-sseo-yo.*
   → You can get to the terminal by subway.

**Your turn:** What can you do? 저는 ___(으)ㄹ 수 있어요. (e.g. 수영할 수 있어요)
