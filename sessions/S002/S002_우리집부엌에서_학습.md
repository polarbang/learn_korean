## How to read this page
- **Sound** = how the word sounds, written in English letters.
- Korean verbs and adjectives change their ending, but they are **still the same word**.
  The **bold part** stays the same — only the ending changes.

### 먹다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **먹**다 | meok-tta |
| Present | **먹**어요 | meo-geo-yo |
| Past | **먹**었어요 | meo-geo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **먹**을 거예요 | meo-geul kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 숟가락으로 김치 볶음밥을 **먹어요**.
   *sut-kka-ra-geu-ro gim-chi bo-kkeum-ba-beul meo-geo-yo.*
   → I eat kimchi fried rice with a spoon.
2. 아침에 빵을 **먹었어요**.
   *a-chi-me ppang-eul meo-geo-sseo-yo.*
   → I ate bread in the morning.
3. 저녁에 친구하고 치킨을 **먹을 거예요**.
   *jeo-nyeo-ge chin-gu-ha-go chi-ki-neul meo-geul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to eat fried chicken with a friend this evening.

### 요리하다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **요리하**다 | yo-ri-ha-da |
| Present | **요리해**요 | yo-ri-hae-yo |
| Past | **요리했**어요 | yo-ri-hae-sseo-yo |
| Future | **요리할** 거예요 | yo-ri-hal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 오늘은 집에서 **요리했어요**.
   *o-neu-reun ji-be-seo yo-ri-hae-sseo-yo.*
   → Today I cooked at home.
2. 저는 주말에 **요리해요**.
   *jeo-neun ju-ma-re yo-ri-hae-yo.*
   → I cook on weekends.
3. 내일 엄마하고 같이 **요리할 거예요**.
   *nae-il eom-ma-ha-go ga-chi yo-ri-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to cook with my mom tomorrow.

### 열다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **열**다 | yeol-da |
| Present | **열**어요 | yeo-reo-yo |
| Past | **열**었어요 | yeo-reo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **열** 거예요 | yeol kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** in the future form, 열 already ends in ㄹ, so it's just **열 거예요** (not 열을). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 냉장고를 **열어요**.
   *naeng-jang-go-reul yeo-reo-yo.*
   → I open the fridge.
2. 방이 더워요. 그래서 창문을 **열었어요**.
   *bang-i deo-wo-yo. geu-rae-seo chang-mu-neul yeo-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → The room is hot. So I opened the window.
3. 선물을 지금 **열 거예요**.
   *seon-mu-reul ji-geum yeol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to open the present now.

### 닫다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **닫**다 | dat-tta |
| Present | **닫**아요 | da-da-yo |
| Past | **닫**았어요 | da-da-sseo-yo |
| Future | **닫**을 거예요 | da-deul kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 밥솥을 **닫아요**.
   *bap-sso-teul da-da-yo.*
   → I close the rice cooker.
2. 추워요. 그래서 문을 **닫았어요**.
   *chu-wo-yo. geu-rae-seo mu-neul da-da-sseo-yo.*
   → It's cold. So I closed the door.
3. 가게는 오늘 일찍 문을 **닫을 거예요**.
   *ga-ge-neun o-neul il-jjik mu-neul da-deul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → The shop will close early today.

### 자르다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **자르**다 | ja-reu-da |
| Present | **잘라**요 | jal-la-yo |
| Past | **잘랐**어요 | jal-la-sseo-yo |
| Future | **자를** 거예요 | ja-reul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 자르 + 아요 becomes **잘라요** (an extra ㄹ appears). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 김치를 **잘라요**.
   *gim-chi-reul jal-la-yo.*
   → I cut the kimchi.
2. 미용실에서 머리를 **잘랐어요**.
   *mi-yong-si-re-seo meo-ri-reul jal-la-sseo-yo.*
   → I got my hair cut at the hair salon.
3. 생일 케이크를 **자를 거예요**.
   *saeng-il ke-i-keu-reul ja-reul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm going to cut the birthday cake.

### 배고프다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **배고프**다 | bae-go-peu-da |
| Present | **배고파**요 | bae-go-pa-yo |
| Past | **배고팠**어요 | bae-go-pa-sseo-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 배고프 + 아요 becomes **배고파요** (the 으 drops). It's still the same word.

In the video you hear **배가 고파요** — the same meaning: 배 (stomach) + 고프다 (empty).

**Examples**
1. 아, **배고파요**. 밥을 먹을 거예요.
   *a, bae-go-pa-yo. ba-beul meo-geul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → Ah, I'm hungry. I'm going to eat.
2. 아침을 안 먹었어요. 그래서 **배고파요**.
   *a-chi-meul an meo-geo-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo bae-go-pa-yo.*
   → I didn't eat breakfast. So I'm hungry.
3. 어제 정말 **배고팠어요**.
   *eo-je jeong-mal bae-go-pa-sseo-yo.*
   → I was really hungry yesterday.

| Past | **좋**았어요 | jo-a-sseo-yo |

   → The weather is nice today.
3. 어제 영화가 정말 **좋았어요**.
   *eo-je yeong-hwa-ga jeong-mal jo-a-sseo-yo.*
   → The movie yesterday was really good.

### 싱겁다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **싱겁**다 | sing-geop-tta |
| Present | **싱거워**요 | sing-geo-wo-yo |
| Past | **싱거웠**어요 | sing-geo-wo-sseo-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 싱겁 + 어요 becomes **싱거워요** (ㅂ changes to 우). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 맛을 봐요. 조금 **싱거워요**.
   *ma-seul bwa-yo. jo-geum sing-geo-wo-yo.*
   → I taste it. It's a little bland.
2. 이 국은 너무 **싱거워요**. 소금을 더 넣어요.
   *i gu-geun neo-mu sing-geo-wo-yo. so-geu-meul deo neo-eo-yo.*
   → This soup is too bland. I add more salt.
3. 어제 찌개는 조금 **싱거웠어요**.
   *eo-je jji-gae-neun jo-geum sing-geo-wo-sseo-yo.*
   → Yesterday's stew was a little bland.

### 뜨겁다

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **뜨겁**다 | tteu-geop-tta |
| Present | **뜨거워**요 | tteu-geo-wo-yo |
| Past | **뜨거웠**어요 | tteu-geo-wo-sseo-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** 뜨겁 + 어요 becomes **뜨거워요** (ㅂ changes to 우). It's still the same word.

뜨겁다 is for things you touch or eat. For hot weather, Korean uses a different word: 덥다.

**Examples**
1. 앗, **뜨거워요**!
   *at, tteu-geo-wo-yo!*
   → Ouch, it's hot!
2. 커피가 너무 **뜨거워요**. 천천히 마셔요.
   *keo-pi-ga neo-mu tteu-geo-wo-yo. cheon-cheon-hi ma-syeo-yo.*
   → The coffee is too hot. I drink it slowly.
3. 아까 국이 정말 **뜨거웠어요**.
   *a-kka gu-gi jeong-mal tteu-geo-wo-sseo-yo.*
   → The soup earlier was really hot.

## 문법 (Grammar): 위에 (on top of)

**위** means "top". Put **위에** right after a noun to say something is (or goes) *on top of* it.

> **\[noun\] 위에** = on (top of) \[noun\]

위에 never changes — it's the same after any noun.

| The egg | on top of the rice | I put |
|---|---|---|
| 계란을 | 밥 **위에** | 올려요 |
| *gye-ra-neul* | *bap wi-e* | *ol-lyeo-yo* |

→ I put the egg on top of the rice.

Same idea: **안에** = inside → 밥솥 **안에** 밥이 있어요. (There's rice inside the rice cooker.)

**Examples**
1. 프라이팬을 가스레인지 **위에** 올려요.
   *peu-ra-i-pae-neul ga-seu-re-in-ji wi-e ol-lyeo-yo.*
   → I put the frying pan on the stove.
2. 그릇이 식탁 **위에** 있어요.
   *geu-reu-si sik-tak wi-e i-sseo-yo.*
   → The bowl is on the table.
3. 핸드폰을 책상 **위에** 놓았어요.
   *haen-deu-po-neul chaek-ssang wi-e no-a-sseo-yo.*
   → I put my phone on the desk.
