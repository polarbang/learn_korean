## Verbs

### 오다 · to come

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *come*.

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **오**다 | o-da |
| Present | **와**요 | wa-yo |
| Past | **왔**어요 | wa-sseo-yo |
| Future | **올** 거예요 | ol kkeo-ye-yo |

Rain "comes" in Korean: 비가 와요 = it's raining.

**Examples**
1. 날씨가 좋아요. 그래서 산에 **왔어요**.
   *nal-ssi-ga jo-a-yo. geu-rae-seo sa-ne wa-sseo-yo.*
   → The weather is nice. So I came to the mountain.
2. 친구가 우리 집에 **와요**.
   *chin-gu-ga u-ri ji-be wa-yo.*
   → My friend comes to my house.
3. 내일 비가 **올 거예요**.
   *nae-il bi-ga ol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → It's going to rain tomorrow.

### 불다 · to blow

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *blow*.

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **불**다 | bul-da |
| Present | **불**어요 | bu-reo-yo |
| Past | **불**었어요 | bu-reo-sseo-yo |
| Future | **불** 거예요 | bul kkeo-ye-yo |

⚠ **Irregular:** in the future form, 불 already ends in ㄹ, so it's just **불 거예요** (not 불을). It's still the same word.

**Examples**
1. 산에 바람이 **불어요**.
   *sa-ne ba-ra-mi bu-reo-yo.*
   → The wind is blowing on the mountain.
2. 어제 바람이 많이 **불었어요**.
   *eo-je ba-ra-mi ma-ni bu-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → It was very windy yesterday.
3. 오늘 밤에 바람이 **불 거예요**.
   *o-neul ba-me ba-ra-mi bul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → It's going to be windy tonight.

3. 내일은 학교까지 **걸을 거예요**.
   *nae-i-reun hak-kkyo-kka-ji geo-reul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → Tomorrow I'll walk to school.

### 막히다 · to be blocked

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *be blocked*.

In the video you hear **막혀 있어요** = it's blocked (and stays blocked). 길이 막혀요 also means *there's a traffic jam*.

   → The roads are really jammed in the morning.
3. 금요일 저녁에는 길이 **막힐 거예요**.
   *geu-myo-il jeo-nyeo-ge-neun gi-ri ma-kil kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → The roads will be jammed on Friday evening.

3. 버스에서 창문 옆에 **앉을 거예요**.
   *beo-seu-e-seo chang-mun yeo-pe an-jeul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll sit by the window on the bus.

### 날아가다 · to fly away

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *fly away*.

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **날아가**다 | na-ra-ga-da |
| Present | **날아가**요 | na-ra-ga-yo |
| Past | **날아갔**어요 | na-ra-ga-sseo-yo |
| Future | **날아갈** 거예요 | na-ra-gal kkeo-ye-yo |

**Examples**
1. 꽃에 가까이 갔어요. 벌이 **날아갔어요**.
   *kko-che ga-kka-i ga-sseo-yo. beo-ri na-ra-ga-sseo-yo.*
   → I went close to the flower. The bee flew away.
2. 새가 하늘로 **날아가요**.
   *sae-ga ha-neul-lo na-ra-ga-yo.*
   → A bird flies away into the sky.
3. 바람이 불어요. 모자가 **날아갈 거예요**!
   *ba-ra-mi bu-reo-yo. mo-ja-ga na-ra-gal kkeo-ye-yo!*
   → It's windy. My hat is going to fly away!

### 감다 · to close (your eyes)

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *close (your eyes)*.

| | Korean | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary form | **감**다 | gam-tta |
| Present | **감**아요 | ga-ma-yo |
| Past | **감**았어요 | ga-ma-sseo-yo |
| Future | **감**을 거예요 | ga-meul kkeo-ye-yo |

감다 is only for eyes (눈을 감다). It also means *to wash your hair*: 머리를 감다.

**Examples**
1. 고양이가 눈을 **감아요**.
   *go-yang-i-ga nu-neul ga-ma-yo.*
   → The cat closes its eyes.
2. 너무 졸려요. 그래서 눈을 **감았어요**.
   *neo-mu jol-lyeo-yo. geu-rae-seo nu-neul ga-ma-sseo-yo.*
   → I'm so sleepy. So I closed my eyes.
3. 저녁에 머리를 **감을 거예요**.
   *jeo-nyeo-ge meo-ri-reul ga-meul kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll wash my hair this evening.

## Adjectives

**Same word, different endings** — all of these mean *cool*.

## Adverbs

Adverbs never change their form.

### 적당히 · just right, moderately

**Sound:** jeok-ttang-hi

**Examples**
1. 바람이 **적당히** 불어요.
   *ba-ra-mi jeok-ttang-hi bu-reo-yo.*
   → The wind is blowing just right.
2. 커피는 **적당히** 마셔요.
   *keo-pi-neun jeok-ttang-hi ma-syeo-yo.*
   → I drink coffee in moderation.
3. 소금을 **적당히** 넣었어요.
   *so-geu-meul jeok-ttang-hi neo-eo-sseo-yo.*
   → I added just the right amount of salt.

### 딱 · exactly, just right

**Sound:** ttak

**Examples**
1. 오늘 날씨가 **딱** 좋아요.
   *o-neul nal-ssi-ga ttak jo-a-yo.*
   → The weather today is just perfect.
2. 이 신발이 저한테 **딱** 맞아요.
   *i sin-ba-ri jeo-han-te ttak ma-ja-yo.*
   → These shoes fit me perfectly.
3. 지금 **딱** 세 시예요.
   *ji-geum ttak se si-ye-yo.*
   → It's exactly three o'clock now.

### 계속 · continuously, keep (doing)

**Sound:** gye-sok

**Examples**
1. **계속** 오르막길이에요.
   *gye-sok o-reu-mak-kki-ri-e-yo.*
   → It keeps going uphill.
2. 아침부터 비가 **계속** 와요.
   *a-chim-bu-teo bi-ga gye-sok wa-yo.*
   → It's been raining since the morning.
3. 내년에도 한국어를 **계속** 공부할 거예요.
   *nae-nyeo-ne-do han-gu-geo-reul gye-sok gong-bu-hal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'll keep studying Korean next year, too.

### 못 · can't

**Sound:** mot

Put 못 right before the verb.

**Examples**
1. 저쪽으로는 **못** 가요.
   *jeo-jjo-geu-ro-neun mot kka-yo.*
   → You can't go that way.
2. 저는 수영을 **못** 해요.
   *jeo-neun su-yeong-eul mo tae-yo.*
   → I can't swim.
3. 어제 너무 바빴어요. 그래서 밥을 **못** 먹었어요.
   *eo-je neo-mu ba-ppa-sseo-yo. geu-rae-seo ba-beul mon meo-geo-sseo-yo.*
   → I was so busy yesterday. So I couldn't eat.

### 지금 · now

**Sound:** ji-geum

지금 = right now, at this moment.

**Examples**
1. **지금** 제 위치를 확인해요.
   *ji-geum je wi-chi-reul hwa-gin-hae-yo.*
   → I check where I am now.
2. **지금** 몇 시예요?
   *ji-geum myeot ssi-ye-yo?*
   → What time is it now?
3. 저는 **지금** 집에 있어요.
   *jeo-neun ji-geum ji-be i-sseo-yo.*
   → I'm at home now.

### 이제 · now (from this point on)

**Sound:** i-je

이제 = now, when something changes from here ("OK, now let's…").

**Examples**
1. **이제** 산을 내려가요.
   *i-je sa-neul nae-ryeo-ga-yo.*
   → Now I head down the mountain.
2. 숙제를 다 했어요. **이제** 놀 거예요.
   *suk-jje-reul da hae-sseo-yo. i-je nol kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I finished my homework. Now I'm going to play.

### 가까이 · close, near

**Sound:** ga-kka-i

**Examples**
1. 꽃에 **가까이** 갔어요.
   *kko-che ga-kka-i ga-sseo-yo.*
   → I went close to the flower.
2. 고양이가 **가까이** 와요.
   *go-yang-i-ga ga-kka-i wa-yo.*
   → The cat comes closer.

### 엄청 · super, really (casual)

**Sound:** eom-cheong

엄청 is casual — great with friends. In polite speech, 정말 or 아주 is safer.

**Examples**
1. 고양이가 **엄청** 귀여워요.
   *go-yang-i-ga eom-cheong gwi-yeo-wo-yo.*
   → The cat is super cute.
2. 오늘 **엄청** 추워요.
   *o-neul eom-cheong chu-wo-yo.*
   → It's super cold today.
3. 어제 **엄청** 많이 걸었어요.
   *eo-je eom-cheong ma-ni geo-reo-sseo-yo.*
   → I walked a ton yesterday.

### 그래서 · so, that's why

**Sound:** geu-rae-seo

**Examples**
1. 목이 말라요. **그래서** 커피를 마셔요.
   *mo-gi mal-la-yo. geu-rae-seo keo-pi-reul ma-syeo-yo.*
   → I'm thirsty. So I drink some coffee.
2. 비가 와요. **그래서** 우산을 가져왔어요.
   *bi-ga wa-yo. geu-rae-seo u-sa-neul ga-jeo-wa-sseo-yo.*
   → It's raining. So I brought an umbrella.
3. 피곤해요. **그래서** 일찍 잘 거예요.
   *pi-gon-hae-yo. geu-rae-seo il-jjik jal kkeo-ye-yo.*
   → I'm tired. So I'll go to bed early.

## Grammar Point: 이/가 있어요 (there is)

Use **있어요** to say something *is there* — perfect for a walk, when you point at what you see.
The thing you see takes **이** or **가**: after a final consonant (받침) use **이** (계단**이**), after a vowel use **가** (지도**가**).

> **[noun]이/가 있어요** = There is [noun].

To say *where*, put **[place]에** in front. For the past, use **있었어요** (there was).

| Next to the path | a railing | there is |
|---|---|---|
| 길 옆에 | 난간**이** | 있어요 |
| *gil yeo-pe* | *nan-ga-ni* | *i-sseo-yo* |

→ There's a railing next to the path.

**Examples**
1. 산에 계단**이** 많이 있어요.
   *sa-ne gye-da-ni ma-ni i-sseo-yo.*
   → There are lots of stairs on the mountain.
2. 가방 안에 텀블러**가** 있어요.
   *ga-bang a-ne teom-beul-leo-ga i-sseo-yo.*
   → There's a tumbler in my bag.
3. 집 앞에 고양이**가** 있었어요.
   *jip a-pe go-yang-i-ga i-sseo-sseo-yo.*
   → There was a cat in front of my house.
